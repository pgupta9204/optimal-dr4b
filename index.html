Mechanics of VEX Triangle Rubber Banding

TEAM 10015Z
VEX Team 10015Z — Zero Tolerance
Developed for competitive VEX teams seeking optimal rubber band assistance and minimal motor stall load.

1. Why Triangle Banding Instead of Linear Banding?

On any parallelogram lift (4-bar or 6-bar), gravity exerts a torque proportional to cos(θ): the lift feels heaviest when horizontal (θ = 0° or negative), and lighter when fully extended upright (θ = 65°).

A standard linear rubber band (attached between two points) has a stretch distance that changes almost linearly with angle, often producing too much tension when raised or not enough when folded flat.

Triangle banding routes a closed loop of rubber bands around three posts (e.g. two on the upper arm / tower and one on the lower arm). Because of the triangle inequality and angle change, the triangle's perimeter P(θ) decreases rapidly as the arms open, creating a large rate of change dP/dθ near the bottom and tapering off near the top. By tuning the 3 post coordinates, the virtual-work torque τ_band(θ) = T(λ) · dP/dθ matches -τ_gravity(θ) almost perfectly across the entire range!

2. Why Replace the Bottom Stage with a 6-Bar (DR6-4B)?

In VEX Robotics, sizing constraints (18" × 18" × 18") restrict how long individual C-channels can be. A 6-bar replaces the single long stage with two linked stages (a short lower arm, a middle floating vertical link, and an upper arm).

  • Greater Vertical Reach: Reaches higher than a 4-bar with the same starting horizontal footprint.
  • Compact Stowed Packaging: Folds into a very low starting height while clearing internal mechanisms (like mobile goal intakes or catapults).
  • More Attachment Locations: The 6-bar's middle floating link moves relative to both the tower and the arms, providing ideal mounting posts for triangle banding.

3. VEX Build Tips for Rubber Bands

  • Standoff Spacers: Use smooth nylon spacers or 3/8" aluminum rollers over #8-32 standoffs so bands can slide freely around the triangle vertices without pinching.
  • Screw Joints & Clearances: Always build lift pivots with high-strength shoulder bolts or #8-32 screw joints with Nyloc nuts and Delrin bearing flats. Standoff posts are strictly constrained to be at least 2 holes away (≥ 1.0") from any pivot to avoid conflicting with bearing flat mounting screws, gear bolt circles, and synchronization gears.
  • Band Pre-Stretch: Keep initial stretch λ_min ≥ 1.15× so bands don't go slack or derail off the standoffs when the lift is at the top of its stroke.

4. VEX Hole Pitch & Measurement System

VEX structural C-channels feature square holes spaced at exactly 0.5" on-center pitch. When counting holes, a distance of 0.5" covers 2 holes, 1.0" covers 3 holes, and so forth:

holes = inches × 2 + 1   ⟺   inches = (holes - 1) × 0.5

Use the Inches / Holes switcher in the top bar to freely toggle all linkage dimensions and post positions between fractional inches and integer hole counts.
